10 Copywriting Tips to Keep Your Word Count Lean

By Editorial Team • 8 Min Read

In the world of copywriting, less is often more. With diminishing attention spans, readers want information delivered quickly and efficiently. Writing bloated copy is one of the fastest ways to lose your audience. To keep them engaged, you need to keep your word count lean.

1. Cut the "Fluff" Adverbs

Words like very, really, just, and actually rarely add value to a sentence. Instead of saying "The dog was very big," say "The dog was enormous." Upgrading your verbs and adjectives eliminates the need for weak adverbs, ensuring your writing hits harder and reads faster.

2. Avoid Passive Voice

Passive voice requires more words and sounds weaker. "The report was written by Sarah" (7 words) can be tightened to "Sarah wrote the report" (4 words). Active voice is punchy and direct, pulling the reader into the action rather than letting them observe it from afar.

3. Delete Redundant Phrasing

We often use phrases that repeat the same meaning. "Past history," "added bonus," "end result," and "free gift" are redundancies. A gift is always free; history is always in the past. Delete the extra word. Not only does this save space, but it also makes you sound more authoritative.

4. Replace Phrases with Single Words

Why write "due to the fact that" when you can write "because"? Why write "in the event that" when you can write "if"? Trimming these bloated phrases keeps your word count strictly focused on the message and respects your reader's valuable time.

5. Stick to One Idea per Sentence

Long, meandering sentences with multiple clauses confuse readers. Break long sentences in half. Not only does this reduce unnecessary transition words, but it dramatically improves your Flesch-Kincaid readability score. Give each core idea its own space to breathe.

6. Be Ruthless with 'That'

The word 'that' is frequently overused and can often be omitted entirely without changing the meaning of a sentence. Instead of "I believe that we can win," write "I believe we can win." Do a quick search for 'that' in your draft and see how many you can delete.

7. Use Bullet Points for Lists

If you are writing a paragraph containing three or more connected items, convert it into a bulleted list. This breaks up dense text visually, allowing readers to scan quickly, and often forces you to shorten the phrasing of the list items.

8. Write the Way You Speak (Then Edit)

When we try to sound 'professional', we often end up writing long, convoluted sentences. Instead, draft your copy as if you were explaining the concept to a friend at a coffee shop. Once the core message is down, edit it to remove the colloquialisms while keeping the punchy conversational rhythm.

9. Let Your Copy Rest

Never edit immediately after writing. Step away from your desk, get a glass of water, or wait until the next day. Coming back with fresh eyes will make it blindingly obvious which sentences are dragging and which words are unnecessary.

10. Focus on the Reader's 'Why'

Bloated copy often stems from focusing too much on your own features rather than the reader's benefits. If you find yourself over-explaining how a product works, stop. Shift the focus to why the reader should care, and watch your word count drop while your conversion rate rises.
